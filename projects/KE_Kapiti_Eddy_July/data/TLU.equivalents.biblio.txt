BIBLIOGRAPHY OF CITATIONS FOR WILD HERBIVORE BODYWEIGHTS (TLU EQUIVALENTS)
File Reference: TLU_equivalents.xlsx
Ecosystem Context: East African Savannah Landscapes (Kapiti / Serengeti-Mara)
================================================================================

This document provides full bibliographic citations and notes for the scientific
publications and authoritative resources used to estimate the average bodyweight
(unit mass in kg, representative across age and sex classes in savannah landscapes)
for the 16 wild herbivore species listed in 'TLU_equivalents.xlsx'.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ALPHABETICAL LIST OF REFERENCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Coe, M. J., Cumming, D. H., & Phillipson, J. (1976).
   Biomass and production of large African herbivores in relation to rainfall and primary production.
   Oecologia, 22(4), 341–354.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00345312
   - Applied to:
     * Coke's Hartebeest (Alcelaphus buselaphus cokii): 125 kg (unit weight)
     * Eland (Tragelaphus oryx): 340 kg (unit weight)
     * Grant's Gazelle (Nanger granti): 40 kg (unit weight)
     * Zebra (Equus quagga): 200 kg (unit weight)
   - Note: Foundational paper defining standardized population "unit weights"
     (accounting for whole-herd population age/sex structure) for African
     savannah herbivore biomass and carrying capacity calculations.

2. FitzGibbon, C. D. (1990).
   Why do cheetahs prefer Thomson's gazelles?
   Animal Behaviour, 40(5), 837–845.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0003-3472(05)80984-4
   - Applied to:
     * Thomson's Gazelle (Eudorcas thomsonii): 18 kg (mean unsexed adult/herd weight)
   - Note: Field study in the Serengeti ecosystem providing empirical body mass
     measurements for male (mean ~22-24 kg), female (mean ~17-18 kg), and
     subadult Thomson's gazelles.

3. Haltenorth, T., & Diller, H. (1980).
   A Field Guide to the Mammals of Africa including Madagascar.
   London: William Collins Sons & Co. Ltd. 400 pp.
   - Applied to:
     * Reedbuck / Bohor Reedbuck (Redunca redunca): 45 kg
   - Note: Classic reference on African ungulate morphometrics; Bohor reedbuck
     males range 43–65 kg and females 35–45 kg, yielding a population average
     of approximately 45 kg.

4. Hendrichs, H., & Hendrichs, U. (1971).
   Dikdik und Elefanten: Ökologie und Soziologie zweier afrikanischer Huftiere.
   Munich: R. Piper & Co. Verlag. 173 pp.
   - Applied to:
     * Dik-Dik / Kirk's Dik-dik (Madoqua kirkii): 5 kg
   - Note: Seminal East African field monograph providing detailed empirical
     body mass determinations for Kirk's dik-dik in savanna ecosystems (adult
     mean weight 4.5–5.5 kg).

5. Jarman, P. J. (1974).
   The social organisation of antelope in relation to their ecology.
   Behaviour, 48(3/4), 215–267.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1163/156853974X00345
   - Applied to:
     * Impala (Aepyceros melampus): 43 kg (unsexed herd mean)
   - Note: Comprehensive behavioural and ecological synthesis across African
     antelopes, incorporating extensive Serengeti impala data where adult ewes
     average ~40 kg and rams ~50–60 kg.

6. Kingdon, J. (1997).
   The Kingdon Field Guide to African Mammals.
   San Diego, CA: Academic Press / Harcourt Brace & Company. 464 pp.
   - Applied to:
     * Bush Duiker / Common Duiker (Sylvicapra grimmia): 15 kg
   - Note: Authoritative taxonomic and ecological compendium; common/bush
     duiker adult live weight typically spans 12–25 kg with an unsexed mean
     of ~15 kg across East and Southern African savannah woodlands.

7. Leuthold, W. (1978).
   On the ecology of the gerenuk Litocranius walleri.
   Journal of Animal Ecology, 47(2), 561–580.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/3801
   - Applied to:
     * Gerenuk (Litocranius walleri): 40 kg
   - Note: Definitive empirical study of gerenuk ecology in Tsavo East National
     Park, Kenya. Adult females average ~35 kg and adult males ~43 kg, giving
     an overall population mean of ~40 kg.

8. Leuthold, W. (1979).
   The lesser kudu, Tragelaphus imberbis: social organization and ecology.
   Journal of Zoology, 187(4), 431–456.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1979.tb03379.x
   - Applied to:
     * Lesser Kudu (Tragelaphus imberbis): 70 kg (standard unit mass)
   - Note: Key ecological study in Tsavo National Park, Kenya. Provides the
     standard 70 kg unit mass widely adopted in East African savanna biomass
     evaluations (males 90–105 kg, females 56–70 kg).

9. Pellew, R. A. (1983).
   The feeding ecology of a selective browser, the giraffe (Giraffa camelopardalis tippelskirchi) in the Serengeti.
   Journal of Zoology, 200(1), 141–159.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7998.1983.tb06167.x
   - Applied to:
     * Masai Giraffe (Giraffa camelopardalis tippelskirchi): 750 kg (population unit weight)
   - Note: Long-term ecological research on the Masai giraffe in the Serengeti-Mara
     savannah landscape. While adult bulls reach ~1,200 kg and adult cows ~830 kg,
     the population unit weight taking into account juveniles and subadults is
     standardized at 736–750 kg (cf. Coe et al. 1976; Georgiadis et al. 2007).

10. Simpson, C. D. (1974).
    Ecology of the Zambezi Valley Bushbuck Tragelaphus scriptus ornatus Pocock.
    Ph.D. dissertation, Department of Wildlife and Fisheries Sciences,
    Texas A&M University, College Station, Texas. 274 pp.
    - Applied to:
      * Bushbuck (Tragelaphus scriptus): 45 kg
    - Note: Comprehensive field study of bushbuck morphometrics and ecology;
      adult males average ~50–65 kg and females ~35–45 kg, establishing an
      overall population mean of ~45 kg.

11. Sinclair, A. R. E. (1977).
    The African Buffalo: A Study of Resource Limitation of Populations.
    Wildlife Behavior and Ecology Series.
    Chicago, IL: The University of Chicago Press. 355 pp.
    - Applied to:
      * African Buffalo (Syncerus caffer): 450 kg (population unit weight)
    - Note: Foundational monograph on buffalo population dynamics in the Serengeti
      savannah ecosystem. Adult cows average ~400–450 kg and adult bulls ~550–650 kg;
      the population average unit weight is established at 450 kg.

12. Sinclair, A. R. E., & Norton-Griffiths, M. (Eds.) (1979).
    Serengeti: Dynamics of an Ecosystem.
    Chicago, IL: The University of Chicago Press. 389 pp.
    - Applied to:
      * Wildebeest (Connochaetes taurinus): 140 kg
    - Note: Landmark ecosystem synthesis. Adult female wildebeest average ~140 kg
      and adult males ~180–200 kg; unit weight across age/sex classes in East African
      savannahs is typically cited between 123 kg (Coe et al. 1976) and 140 kg.

13. Skinner, J. D., & Chimimba, C. T. (2005).
    The Mammals of the Southern African Subregion (3rd ed.).
    Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. 814 pp.
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781107340992
    - Applied to:
      * Steinbok / Steenbok (Raphicerus campestris): 11 kg
    - Note: Definitive reference compilation of southern/eastern African mammal
      measurements. Mature male steenbok average ~11.0 kg and females ~11.3 kg
      (range 7–16 kg), giving a representative population mean of 11 kg.
